My Search for Hope and Every Bench in Golden Gate Park

I moved to San Francisco to get out of a rut, but when I arrived I felt uncertain how to live my newly wide-open life. To get to know the city, I took aimless walks through our parks, resting on benches which often featured dedications. I found solace sitting with these tributes to a stranger, imagining their lives, seeing which messages excited me and showed me who I want to be.

Hopelessness frequently overwhelmed me; I wanted a container for it. So, I decided to wind my way through Golden Gate Park to create a map of all the bench dedications, it took a year. I set out with a pencil, a notebook, and a GPS app on my phone (and without any idea how many benches existed). I wanted to use data collection– repetitive, boring, maybe pointless– as a devotional task to steady me as I charted out a new path for myself.

The night before I began, the clocks sprung forward. I celebrated the extension of daylight by watching the sunset on top of Twin Peaks with friends, none of whom I knew a year ago. These kinds of surprises are the antidote to hopelessness. If I couldn’t imagine this a year ago, what else can I not imagine that is coming for me?

Friends and family send me pictures of benches from their city or travels. Luke from San Diego, Katie from Carmel, Liam from New York, my Mom from Wisconsin, Nancy from another corner of San Francisco. Each one lights me up, knowing they are thinking of me.

Feeling so seen and loved– and able to take it in– it’s new for me. Before starting this project, I wondered if I would lead the kind of life that encourages someone to remember me sweetly. I learned I already am.

I hoped for a transformation that felt complete, that demarcated a clear before and after in my life. But, I don’t need that anymore. Hopelessness, failure, loneliness, they are not currently defining my life, but they may visit again.

I am not scared, not while sitting on a bench and staring out into the world.
